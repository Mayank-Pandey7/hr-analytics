Executive Retention Strategy & Management Action Plan

Based on rigorous empirical validation across 326 employees, attrition is not caused by poor performance or lack of promotions. Rather, it is concentrated in an unsustainable operational interaction between overtime workload and sub-market entry compensation.

1

Workload Governance & Overtime Normalization

Overtime drives attrition from 16.4% to 56.4% (3.4x multiplier).

  • Implement a hard cap of 8 overtime hours per week per employee.
  • Provide 1:1 compensatory off (comp-off) days for overtime within 30 days.
  • Add shift coverage for Laboratory Technicians and Sales Reps.
2

Entry-Level Compensation Realignment

Attrition in the <$3,500 band is 41.7% (median leaver salary: $2,515 vs $3,375).

  • Raise base entry salaries for Sales Reps and Lab Techs to $3,200-$3,500 threshold.
  • Restructure Sales compensation to increase fixed base pay by 15%.
  • Conduct annual compensation parity audits against regional benchmarks.
3

First-Year Onboarding & Mentorship

46.2% of staff in their first year at the company depart.

  • Pair young new hires (<25) with tenured mentors (>5 yrs tenure).
  • Schedule formal 30-60-90 day check-ins led by HR Business Partners.
  • Clarify promotional milestones and technical career tracks early.
4

HR Division Administrative Restructuring

Human Resources suffered the highest attrition rate of any division (46.2%).

  • Deploy HR ticketing software to reduce repetitive manual documentation.
  • Adjust HR Specialist compensation to market parity ($3,500+).
  • Implement quarterly anonymous workload pulse surveys.
